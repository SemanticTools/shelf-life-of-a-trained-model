# Why LLMs Need to Change

*The shelf life of a trained model, and the case for separating knowledge from computation*

D.W. Murray / Sigmantics AB · v1.0 · 6 June 2026

## Summary

A trained LLM has a shelf life of roughly a year. Its knowledge is frozen at the training cutoff and cannot be cheaply updated, because knowledge and computation are fused into the same weights. The only reliable way to stay current is to retrain, which makes the training run a recurring subscription rather than a durable asset. The fix is to separate knowledge from computation into an addressable, updatable layer.

**Claims:** an LLM's world-knowledge ages within about a year and cannot be cheaply updated; the architecture should separate knowledge from computation.

**Does not claim:** that LLMs are a dead end, that scaling is pointless, or that a finished alternative exists.
